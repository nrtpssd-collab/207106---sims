Charging Processes

207106 · Lecture 01 · Electric Charge and Field · Live simulation 2

Charging by contact

A charged rod touches a neutral metal sphere. Electrons move across the contact point, then spread over the sphere's surface.
Look for: the sphere ends up with the same sign as the rod. The charge sits on the outer surface and spreads uniformly once the rod is gone. Total charge (rod + sphere) never changes.

Charging by induction

Nothing touches the sphere. The rod only pushes the free electrons around, and a ground wire lets some leave or arrive.
Ask: after step 4, is the sphere charged with the same sign as the rod or the opposite sign? Before grounding, why is a neutral sphere still attracted to the rod?

Polarization of an insulator

In an insulator the electrons cannot leave their atoms. Each electron cloud only shifts slightly, so every atom becomes a tiny dipole.
Try: switch the sign of the rod. The clouds flip direction, but the force is attractive both times. The near side always carries charge opposite to the rod's, and it is closer. This is how a charged comb picks up bits of paper.

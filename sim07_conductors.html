A neutral conductor in a uniform field

The field is computed by solving Laplace's equation numerically, with the conductor held at one potential and its total charge kept at zero.
Look for: E = 0 everywhere inside (white). Field lines meet the surface at right angles. Induced − charge on the side facing the incoming field, + on the other side, total zero. Drag the conductor around.

Where does excess charge go?

The conductor carries a net positive charge and there is no external field.
Look for: all the charge sits on the surface, and it crowds at sharp points, where the field just outside (σ/ε₀) is strongest. This is why lightning rods are pointed, and why sharp edges on high-voltage equipment spark.

Shielding: a hollow conductor

Look for: the cavity stays field-free however strong E₀ is. The charges rearrange on the outer surface only. Cut a gap: the field leaks in a little, but only near the opening.
